# O*NET & VOCATIONAL DECISION STANDARDS: CAREER DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE BASE

## 1. Skill Acquisition & Competency Gaps
- When transitioning roles (e.g., individual contributor to engineering management, or backend engineer to ML engineer), required competencies fall into three distinct layers:
  1. Core Technical Competencies: Directly required tools, frameworks, and system knowledge (e.g., PyTorch, Kubernetes, SQL).
  2. Procedural & Domain Knowledge: Architectural workflows, deployment lifecycles, regulatory or domain constraints (e.g., HIPAA, FINRA).
  3. Leadership & Soft Competencies: Cross-functional communication, roadmap prioritization, performance evaluations, conflict mediation.
- Transition Feasibility Rule: A lateral transition within the same technical domain typically requires 3–6 months of bridging. A vertical transition into management or an orthogonal pivot (e.g., software to medicine) requires a 12–36 month credential or apprenticeship pipeline.

## 2. Financial Runway & Opportunity Cost Analysis
- Sunk Cost vs. Forward Runway: Career decisions must not be justified by past time or money spent in an existing field (Sunk Cost Fallacy).
- Minimum Safe Financial Runway Formula: Safe Runway = Total Liquid Savings / Monthly Core Burn Rate.
  - Safe career pivot threshold: Minimum 6 months of baseline living expenses without debt accumulation.
  - Startup/entrepreneurship pivot threshold: Minimum 12–18 months of personal living runway plus initial capital expenditure.
- Total Compensation Deconstruction:
  - Base Salary: Guaranteed liquid cash flow for immediate living expenses.
  - Variable Bonus: Performance-dependent cash flow (discount at 50% for risk assessment).
  - Equity & Stock Grants: RSUs (public liquid stock) vs. ISO/NSO Options (private illiquid equity with 83(b) tax and strike price exercise capital requirements).
  - Acceleration Clauses: Single-trigger vs. Double-trigger acceleration on acquisition or termination.

## 3. Burnout, Sustainability & Work-Life Trade-offs
- Occupational Burnout Vectors:
  - Weekly Workload: Working consistently >55 hours per week increases health risks and impairs analytical decision quality.
  - Operational On-Call Burden: High pager duty frequency directly correlates with job dissatisfaction and early resignation.
  - Autonomy vs. Micromanagement: High responsibility with low authority is the leading driver of managerial burnout.
- Long-term Marketability vs. Short-term Compensation:
  - Taking a higher-paying role in a dying tech stack decreases future market value.
  - Prioritize roles that enhance portfolio visibility, network density, and transferrable problem-solving capital.
