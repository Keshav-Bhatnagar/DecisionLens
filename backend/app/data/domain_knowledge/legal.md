# COMMON PAPER & COMMERCIAL CONTRACT STANDARDS: LEGAL DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE BASE

## 1. Commercial Contract Risk Factors & Liability Ceilings
- Aggregate Liability Caps:
  - Standard industry practice for B2B SaaS and service agreements is to cap total liability at the total fees paid or payable by the customer in the preceding 12-month period.
  - Dangerous Clause: Uncapped liability or liability tied to gross revenue creates catastrophic existential risk.
  - Carve-outs from Liability Caps: Common mutual carve-outs include gross negligence, willful misconduct, breaches of confidentiality, and third-party IP indemnification obligations.
- Indemnification & Defense Obligations:
  - Third-party Intellectual Property Infringement: The vendor must defend and indemnify the customer against claims that the software infringes a patent, copyright, or trade secret.
  - Customer Indemnification: Should strictly be limited to customer data illegality or breach of acceptable use policies.

## 2. Restrictive Covenants & Post-Employment Provisions
- Non-Compete Clauses:
  - United States (California): Post-employment non-competes are void as a matter of public policy under California Business & Professions Code § 16600. Employers cannot legally enforce them regardless of choice of law.
  - United States (FTC Rule / General): Heavily scrutinized; must be strictly limited in geographic scope, duration (typically <= 12 months), and demonstrable trade secret protection.
  - India: Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act renders post-employment non-compete covenants void and unenforceable as a restraint of trade.
- Non-Solicitation of Employees:
  - Must specify a reasonable duration (typically 6–12 months) and define target employees as those directly engaged in material business activities.
- Intellectual Property Assignment & Moonlighting:
  - Standard employment agreements claim ownership of inventions created during employment.
  - Statutory Exception (e.g., California Labor Code § 2870): Protects employee inventions developed entirely on own time without using employer equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secret information.

## 3. Termination, Exit & Data Governance
- Termination for Convenience:
  - Unilateral termination for convenience by a customer without an early termination fee leaves vendors with unrecouped infrastructure costs.
  - Mutual termination for cause requires a mandatory 30-day written cure period.
- Data Portability & Post-Termination Transition:
  - Contracts must explicitly define data retrieval formats (JSON/CSV), deletion certification timelines (typically 30–60 days), and transition assistance rates.
